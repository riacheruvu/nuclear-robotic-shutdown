Why numbers may differ slightly
RNG streams differ; browser uses fewer rollouts; full 7-D IS joint weights and GPU batching live in Python. The dynamics and dose laws are intentionally the same structure.

Plain-English model

The robot is a little wheeled vehicle that always tries to point at the valve and drive forward. Commands take two time steps to affect motion (teleoperation lag). Every step the true position may slip a little, and occasionally the sensor reports a wild wrong \((x,y)\). Closer to the valve (hot source) → much higher dose rate (inverse-square).

\(\dot D(r)=\Gamma A/r^{2}\cdot e^{-\mu r}+D_{\mathrm{bg}}\)   then per-second model rate \(=\dot D/3600\)
Control: \(e=\mathrm{wrap}(\mathrm{atan2}(-y,-x)-\theta),\ \omega=K_p e,\ v=V(1-0.5|e|/\pi)\)
Lag: motion at \(t\) uses \(u_{t-2}\). Failure: dose \(>D_{\max}\) or max path deviation \(>R_{\mathrm{safe}}\).

Assumptions (read before trusting numbers)

01
Point isotropic source

No walls, shielding, or scattering. Teaching field, not facility geometry.

02
Simplified dose units

Same research convention as remote_qual.core.units. Labels are dose-like model units for thresholds — not a regulatory dosimetry product.

03
Fixed 2-step lag

No random jitter or packet loss in this demo (easy extension).

04
Empty plane

Corridor is distance to the nominal path, not CAD obstacles.

05
Reachability is approximate

Axis-aligned linearized boxes on the browser; can bloat. Python module is the reportable engine.

06
Research ≠ certification

Use for intuition and teaching. Plant decisions need qualified codes and procedures.
